Does fixing one bias move it somewhere else? PROTOTYPE

Model tested:

The idea

AI language tools learn from huge amounts of text, and they absorb its stereotypes. Some social groups end up associated with positive words (like "good" or "competent") and others with negative ones.

One proposed fix is to remove one kind of bias from the model, for example racial bias. This page tests a worry: does fixing one kind of bias make bias toward other groups worse?

How to read the numbers. Each group (for example "poor" or "Muslim") gets a score: higher means the model links the group more with positive words, lower means more with negative words. Within a category (like Religion), the gap is the score of the most favored group minus the score of the least favored one. A bigger gap means a more unequal treatment.

Scores are small numbers (roughly −0.15 to +0.15). A change under 0.005 is too small to treat as real, so we call it "barely changed".

Keep in mind

  • This is a prototype, not a research finding. It uses one small AI model, one way of removing bias, and only 4 to 25 words per group. We have not tested whether the changes are statistically meaningful.
  • Ignore the category we fixed. Its gap always drops to about zero, because we removed it on purpose. What matters is what happens to everything else.
  • Small bars are not meaningful. Anything inside the grey "about no change" zone should be read as no change.
  • This model has no gender category. The 72 groups come from a published list (Charlesworth et al. 2023) that doesn't include it.

Technical details and glossary

Embedding
The model's internal "map" of word meanings. Each word is a point in a 100-dimensional space; words used in similar contexts sit near each other.
Score
Average similarity of a group's words to the positive words minus its average similarity to the negative words (cosine similarity). Positive words: . Negative words: .
Gap
Highest group score minus lowest group score within one category.
Removing a bias (k)
We find the directions in the map that separate the groups of one category, then delete them from every word (a "subspace subtraction", after Bolukbasi et al. 2016). k is the number of directions deleted; "full rank" means all of them, one fewer than the number of distinct groups.
Group names
"old (vs. young)" is the word list for old, defined against young, as in Charlesworth et al. (2023). Some groups share identical word lists; they stay in the tables but are merged into one dot in the network and counted once when finding the directions to remove. This is still to be confirmed with the original authors.
